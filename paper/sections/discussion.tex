\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why per-query weighting is hard to learn}
We expected identifier-heavy queries to want a low dense weight and cheap
features to detect them. The first half holds at the collection level, since
the RFC sweep favours the sparse side; the second does not, as the identifier
share carries almost no signal. We see three reasons. Many training queries
have the same nDCG@10 at every weight, presumably because both retrievers
already rank a relevant passage first or neither finds one, so their targets
are uninformative. The oracle is defined on a coarse grid of a rank-based
fusion, where small rank changes can move the longest plateau, so the targets
are likely noisy. And the model was trained on SciFact and FiQA, where the
dense side is usually right, never on text like RFC. A predictor with some
in-domain labels, or with post-retrieval evidence as in DAT~\cite{hsu2025dat},
may close more of the headroom; pre-retrieval lexical statistics alone did not.

\subsection{Cost and deployment guidance}
Three practical points follow. Sweep $\alpha$ on a small labelled sample of
the target collection before fixing it, since a weight chosen on other domains
landed on the wrong side of the curve for RFC. Keep BM25 for identifier-heavy
text even with a strong dense encoder. Measure the reranker before adopting
it: on our laptop it multiplies latency many times over and lowered nDCG@10
where the first stage was already strong. QAF costs well under a millisecond,
but on our evidence it does not replace tuning.

\subsection{Limitations}
Several choices limit how far our results generalise. All experiments run on
subsets (FiQA 20{,}000 passages, HotpotQA 500 questions with their own
paragraphs as the corpus, RFC 101 questions), so absolute scores are not
comparable with full-benchmark numbers (Section~\ref{sec:results}). The RFC questions were drafted by the same model
(Qwen2.5-7B) that later generates answers, filtered automatically, screened with AI
assistance and then verified by all three authors. Every row was checked, but
the set still favours questions that a 7B model finds easy to write, namely
short identifier lookups, and it has no multi-hop items. RFC~2616, 7230 and
9110/9112 are successive HTTP/1.1 specifications with overlapping text, so
retrieving a near-duplicate passage from a superseded RFC counts as a miss.
Questions whose answer was ambiguous across RFCs were removed during
verification, but this scoring effect remains for the rest.

QAF was fitted on scientific and financial dev queries only. % NOTE: revise the next sentence if the DAT-style baseline (Task 16) is run.
We compare it with a fixed weight and the oracle but not with an LLM-based
scheme such as DAT, so we cannot say how much accuracy the LLM-free design
gives up. Answers and their faithfulness are judged by the same local 7B model,
so we treat RAGAS scores only as a relative signal. Latencies come from one
laptop and will differ elsewhere in absolute terms. We also cannot rule out
that EmbeddingGemma~2 saw SciFact or HotpotQA text during training, which would
flatter its dense scores there.
