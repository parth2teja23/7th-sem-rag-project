\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textit{Sparse and dense retrieval.} BM25~\cite{robertson2009bm25} remains a
strong baseline; on BEIR~\cite{thakur2021beir} it beats many neural
retrievers outside their training domain. Dense retrievers address its
vocabulary mismatch: DPR~\cite{karpukhin2020dpr} trained a BERT bi-encoder
that outperformed BM25 on open-domain QA, Sentence-BERT~\cite{reimers2019sbert}
made such encoders easy to train, and ColBERT~\cite{khattab2020colbert} keeps
one vector per token to recover some exact matching at the cost of a larger
index. Among general-purpose embedders, BGE~\cite{xiao2024cpack} is small and
strong on English, while EmbeddingGemma~\cite{vera2025embeddinggemma} and its
multimodal successor EmbeddingGemma~2~\cite{vera2026embeddinggemma2} target
on-device use and are trained with Matryoshka representation
learning~\cite{kusupati2022matryoshka}, so their embeddings can be truncated.

\textit{Hybrid retrieval and fusion.} Reciprocal Rank
Fusion~\cite{cormack2009rrf} merges ranked lists using ranks alone.
Kuzi et al.~\cite{kuzi2020hybrid} showed that lexical and semantic matching
find different relevant documents, so running both raises recall, and
Pyserini~\cite{lin2021pyserini} made sparse, dense and hybrid runs
reproducible. Bruch et al.~\cite{bruch2023fusion} found that a tuned convex
combination of normalised scores can beat RRF and that RRF is sensitive to
its parameters. All of these use one setting for every query. DAT~\cite{hsu2025dat},
the closest work to ours, asks an LLM to rate the top-1 dense and sparse
results of each query and turns the ratings into the fusion weight. It
reports gains over fixed weighting, at the price of LLM calls on the
retrieval path. QAF targets the same decision with a linear model over cheap
statistics and no model call at query time.

\textit{Reranking and RAG.} A cross-encoder runs a full transformer pass per
query--passage pair, so its cost grows with the number of candidates, whereas
a bi-encoder embeds passages once, offline; Nogueira and
Cho~\cite{nogueira2019passage} therefore applied BERT only to a short
first-stage list, the pattern we follow. DrQA~\cite{chen2017drqa} paired a
TF-IDF retriever with a neural reader, RAG~\cite{lewis2020rag} trained a
dense retriever and generator together, and Gao et al.~\cite{gao2023ragsurvey}
survey later variants. Ma et al.~\cite{ma2023rewriting} rewrite queries before
retrieval; our optional expansion is a simpler, untrained form of that idea.
RAGAS~\cite{es2024ragas} scores faithfulness with an LLM judge.
PageIndex~\cite{vectify2025pageindex} drops both indexes and lets an LLM
navigate a table-of-contents tree of each document, which suits structured
texts such as RFCs but costs several LLM calls per query.
